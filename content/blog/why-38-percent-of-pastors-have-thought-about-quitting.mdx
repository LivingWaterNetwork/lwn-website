Barna Group's research on pastoral well-being found that **38% of U.S. pastors have considered quitting full-time ministry within the past year** — a number that jumped nine points in a single year during a season of unusual strain. Even more sobering: **only 35% of pastors qualify as fully "healthy,"** meaning they score well across all six categories Barna measures — spiritual, physical, emotional, and vocational health among them.

That's not a story about weak leaders. It's a story about leaders who were sent to pour out before anyone made sure they had anything left to pour.

## The Pattern Isn't Limited to Pulpits

We work with pastors, but we also work with marketplace leaders, nonprofit founders, and volunteers carrying more than their title suggests — and the pattern is identical. The people everyone else brings their needs to rarely have anyone bringing that same care back to them.

Barna's data even breaks down who's most at risk: pastors under 45 are considering quitting at a higher rate (46%) than those over 45 (34%), and pastors who've been in ministry roughly 20 years but at their current church for closer to 7 show up disproportionately in the "considering resignation" group. In other words — this isn't a young-and-inexperienced problem. It's a *depleted* problem, and depletion doesn't check your résumé first.
