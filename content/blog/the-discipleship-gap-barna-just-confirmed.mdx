Barna Group's research on discipleship engagement found something that should stop every church leader mid-scroll: **39% of Christians are not engaged in discipleship in any direction** — not being discipled, and not discipling anyone else. Only 28% are doing both at once.

That's not a fringe statistic. That's nearly two out of every five Christians sitting in a pew somewhere, disconnected from the one practice Jesus actually commanded: *make disciples* (Matthew 28:19).

## It's Not Apathy. It's a Confidence Gap.

The part of Barna's research that should reshape how we respond isn't the 39% — it's *why*. The top barrier Christians report isn't disinterest. It's this: **37% say they don't feel qualified or equipped to disciple someone else.** Another 24% say no one has ever asked them.

Read that again. More than a third of Christians would be willing to disciple someone — they just don't think they're allowed to, or nobody has invited them into it.

That's not a heart problem. That's a structure problem. And structure is fixable.

> "Nobody discipled me because I finally felt qualified. Somebody discipled me because they were willing to sit with an unfinished person and call it worth their time. That's the whole model — not qualification, availability."
>
> — Omar J. Fandino, Founder of Living Water Network · *echoes 2 Timothy 2:2*

## The Invitation Barna's Data Points To

If you've read this far and quietly thought, *I don't feel qualified either* — you're not alone, and you're not disqualified. You're exactly who this is for. Formation isn't a credential you earn before you start; it's what happens while you're walking with someone else.

The 39% doesn't have to include you next year.
