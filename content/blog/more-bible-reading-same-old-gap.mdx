Barna's newest tracking data contains one of the more encouraging numbers we've seen in years: **weekly Bible reading among Gen Z rose from 30% to 49% in a single year** — the largest jump of any generation Barna tracks. Millennials saw a nearly identical surge, from 34% to 50%. For the first time in over a decade, young adults are now reading Scripture weekly at rates that rival or beat older generations.

That's genuinely good news. It's also, if we're honest, not the whole story.

## Reading More, Believing the Same

In the same research, Barna found that **only 36% of all Americans — and just 44% of Christians — strongly affirm that the Bible is totally accurate in all the principles it teaches.** Scripture engagement is climbing. Confidence that Scripture is actually true and formative is not climbing at the same rate.

That gap is not a contradiction. It's exactly what happens when *information* increases faster than *formation*. You can read more and still not be more convinced that what you're reading is meant to reshape how you actually live.
