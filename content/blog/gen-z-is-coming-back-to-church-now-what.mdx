For most of the last two decades, the story about young adults and church has been a story of decline. Barna's most recent tracking data flips that narrative on its head: **the typical Gen Z churchgoer now attends 1.9 weekends a month — more often than Millennials (1.8), and more often than the churched-adult average of 1.6.** In 2020, that same generation was averaging closer to one weekend a month. Attendance has nearly doubled in five years.

Something is shifting. A generation that was supposed to be walking away is, in measurable numbers, walking back in.

## Attendance Is Not the Same as Formation

Here's the tension every leader needs to sit with before celebrating too quickly: showing up on Sunday and being formed into Christlikeness are not the same metric, and Barna's own discipleship research makes that painfully clear — 39% of Christians, across every generation, aren't engaged in discipleship at all. A generation can fill more seats and still not be *formed* in any meaningful way, if all that's waiting for them once they arrive is another service to attend rather than a relationship to grow in.

Gen Z showing up is an open door. What happens on the other side of that door is the actual question.

## The Real Opportunity

Gen Z returning to church is genuinely good news — Barna's data is clear about that. But if the Church's only response is louder music and a better lobby, this generation will do what every generation before it has done when formation wasn't on offer: they'll show up for a while, and then they'll leave again, just a little more disillusioned than before.

The door is open. What we build behind it is what determines whether this is a moment or a movement.
