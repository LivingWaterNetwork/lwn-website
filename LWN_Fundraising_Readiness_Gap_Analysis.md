# Living Water Network — Fundraising Readiness Gap Analysis

*Prepared July 2026. Comparison benchmark: Denver Institute for Faith & Work (DIFW), a similarly-sized faith-and-leadership-formation nonprofit, plus general ECFA/GuideStar transparency norms for faith-based organizations.*

## Where the site already earns credibility

The current build already does several things right for an organization at pre-launch stage: a full Theory of Change page with a five-stage logic model, EIN and 501(c)(3) status stated on multiple pages, an honest program-cost breakdown that now includes staffing, a founder story on the About page, three blog posts, and a working donation/contact pipeline. That's a stronger foundation than most first-year nonprofits have at this point.

## The comparable: Denver Institute for Faith & Work

DIFW is a useful benchmark because it does the same kind of work you do — forming leaders through a faith lens — at a scale you're aiming for within a few years. Founded by Jeff Haanen in 2012, it's ECFA-accredited, publishes annual reports (its 2023 and 2024 reports are both public at difw.org), and its site names its CEO, VP of Operations & Finance, and VP of Formation individually. None of that existed in DIFW's first year either — it's the result of a decade of building. The comparison isn't "why don't we look like this yet," it's "here's the order they built it in, and we can follow the same sequence."

## Gaps, sorted by what's realistic now vs. later

**Realistic to add now — no press, no board, no track record required:**

A founding story / timeline section works even at year zero — when and why LWN was founded, the personal story behind it, and what the first cohort will look like. You have this material; it just isn't laid out as a distinct "how we started" narrative yet, separate from the founder bio.

A one-page financial transparency statement — EIN, fiscal year, current 990 status ("first return will be filed for FY2026" is a legitimate, honest thing to say), and a stated commitment to publish an annual report after Year 1 closes — signals intent to be accountable before you have anything to show yet. Cheaper and faster than pursuing ECFA now.

A "How We Steward Every Dollar" expansion — you already have the percentage breakdown on /donate; consider adding one sentence naming the actual budget total for Year 1 ($145,000) so a donor can see the whole picture, not just percentages of an unstated number.

**Worth planning for, not urgent:**

ECFA accreditation is the single strongest trust signal in this space, but it isn't cheap or fast — fees run $730–$19,100/year scaled to donated income, and it requires a full financial review including board minutes and a conflict-of-interest policy, which presumes a functioning board. This is a Year 2 milestone, once the board is seated and you have a full fiscal year of financials to submit. Note it as a stated goal on the site now ("pursuing ECFA accreditation as we complete our first fiscal year") rather than waiting to mention it until you've already achieved it — donors respond well to a stated roadmap.

GuideStar/Candid's Seal of Transparency is a lower bar than ECFA — free registration plus basic organizational and financial info — and can realistically be completed within the first year. Worth doing as soon as you have even one year of numbers to report.

An annual report is the highest-leverage single document you can produce, and it's the thing DIFW, and virtually every credible faith-based nonprofit, leads with. You can't publish one with integrity before Year 1 closes, but you can commit to it publicly now, which itself builds trust ("Our first Annual Report will be published in [month] 2027").

**Deliberately not recommended yet:**

A public team/leadership page beyond the founder bio doesn't make sense with two staff and no board — it would read as padding. Revisit once you have 2–3 more hires or the board is seated (and even then, per your instruction, the board itself stays private/invite-only rather than a public page).

Press mentions, partner logos, and testimonials can't be fabricated or rushed — leave placeholders out entirely rather than using generic stock-photo "testimonial" sections, which read as inauthentic to donors who've seen enough nonprofit sites to spot it. The most credible thing a pre-launch nonprofit can do is be honest that it's pre-launch.

## Bottom line

The site doesn't need more features to function as a pitch deck right now — it needs the founding-story section and the financial-transparency/roadmap statement, both of which can be written from material you already have. ECFA, GuideStar's Seal, and the annual report are the real next tier of credibility-building, but they're sequenced to specific milestones (seated board, closed fiscal year) rather than things to rush before those milestones exist. Naming them as a stated roadmap on the site costs nothing and signals the same seriousness DIFW's site now reflects after over a decade of doing exactly that.
